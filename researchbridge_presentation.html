The Problem
Great research.
Nobody understands it.
47.6%
of adults surveyed across 8 EU member states have limited health literacy — making research findings inaccessible to nearly half the population
HLS-EU Consortium, Eur J Public Health, 2015 · PMC4668324
No training
Scientists are thoroughly trained in research methods but typically receive no explicit training in communicating to lay audiences
Brownell et al., J Undergrad Neurosci Educ, 2013 · PMC3852879
#1 barrier
Lack of formal communication training is among the most commonly cited barriers to public science engagement
Swords et al., J Microbiol Biol Educ, 2023 · PMC10443307
The problem is not the research. It's the translation.
Why It Matters
In health, poor communication
has real consequences
📢
Misinformation
Anti-scientific misinformation has grown sharply in recent years, posing a direct risk to public health, political stability and environmental sustainability.
Swords et al., JMBE, 2023 · PMC10443307
📋
Policy Disconnect
Research briefs are routinely described by decision-makers as "too technical" to act on. Research format and language were identified as barriers in 18 independent studies of evidence use by policymakers.
Miller & Piver-Renna, IES Blog, 2022 · Lavis et al., PMC3909454
🏥
Worse Health Outcomes
Lower health literacy correlates with worse self-reported health status and higher demand for health services — a cycle that inaccessible research outputs reinforce across EU populations.
HLS-EU Consortium, Eur J Public Health, 2015 · PMC4668324

"The essential attributes of effective science communicators can be summarized by the 'three As': accessible, accountable, and adaptable."
— Nana & Bhatt, Nature Neuroscience, 2024 · PMC12265990

The Root Cause
Researchers are trained to produce science.
Not to explain it.
✓ What PhD training covers
✅Research methodology & study design
✅Statistical analysis
✅Scientific writing for peer review
✅Conference presentations
✗ What PhD training rarely covers
❌Plain language communication
❌Visual design for non-expert audiences
❌Audience-adapted data presentation
❌Science communication for the public

User Research Plan
Understanding the user before building the solution
Evidence shows that few scientific training programs teach the skills needed to communicate with nonexperts — making it essential to understand what researchers actually need before building a tool to help them. Swords et al., J Microbiol Biol Educ, 2023 · PMC10443307
1
Qualitative · Semi-structured Interviews
N = 5–8 PhD researchers / postdocs · HEADS programme + MEDCIDS · Next 2 weeks
  • How do you currently share findings with non-specialist audiences?
  • What tools do you use? What frustrates you?
  • Have you ever simplified your work for a patient or policymaker?
  • What would an ideal communication tool look like?
2
Quantitative · Online Survey
N = 25–40 health researchers across health sciences · Weeks 3–4
  • Self-rated confidence in data visualisation
  • Frequency of non-specialist communication
  • Current tools used and perceived gaps
  • Biggest perceived barriers to communication
Interview N=5–8 follows Nielsen (2000) — 5 participants surface ~85% of usability issues · Survey N=25–40 follows established science communication survey design · Swords et al., 2023
