What makes something invisible?

It is not captured.

Governance · Can it cross the sector boundary?

Evaluative · Is it measured?

It is not valued.

Invisibility is not only a property of reality.

BVDL · BMHI Visibility Hypothesis Board

Available is not the same as visible

A determinant becomes visible only when it passes through every step below. Visibility therefore needs an end-to-end infrastructure, from data acquisition to action.

1 · Captured

The determinant enters a digital system.

→

2 · Represented

It is recorded in a defined, computable form.

→

3 · Exchanged

It can travel between systems and sectors.

→

4 · Interpreted

The receiving system understands what it means.

→

5 · Acted on

It reaches the decision it should inform.

One BMHI response per layer

INFORMATIONAL

Open new data-acquisition channels: sensors, citizen-generated data, environmental monitoring.

SEMANTIC

Apply common terminologies, ontologies and standards such as SNOMED CT, LOINC and FHIR.

EXPERIENTIAL

Turn narratives, patient-reported outcomes and social context into computable information.

GOVERNANCE

Establish the legal, organisational and technical mechanisms for exchange across sectors.

EVALUATIVE

Create indicators that measure contextual determinants in routine monitoring.

ETL · European Translation Lab

Why Europe?

This challenge is particularly relevant in Europe. Europe combines universal healthcare systems, strong commitments to equity, cross-border collaboration, the European Health Data Space, mature interoperability initiatives and leadership in digital governance.

A UNIQUE OPPORTUNITY

To become the first region capable of systematically integrating clinical, social, environmental and digital determinants into health intelligence.

The causes cross borders

People migrate, pollution spreads and diseases pass between animals and people.

The skills gap is too big for one country

WHO warns of a growing health-worker shortage alongside a widening digital-skills gap.

Fair health goals depend on it

Neither EU health-equity objectives nor the UN Sustainable Development Goals can be met while social and environmental context stays invisible.

The timing is right

The EHDS sets a common framework for health records; the AI Act requires representative data with human oversight.

Reg. (EU) 2022/2371 [63]; WHO Europe [64, 65]; UN [66]; AI Act [67]; EHDS, Reg. (EU) 2025/327 [69]

ETL · Most urgent

Act before context-blind systems become the norm.

The AI Act classifies most medical AI as high-risk and requires high-quality, representative data with human oversight.

EHR systems lack fields for contextual data, and models trained on context-poor data replicate invisibility.

Sjoding et al. [60]; Obermeyer et al. [61]; AI Act, Reg. (EU) 2024/1689 [67]

For decades we focused on making the invisible visible.

That remains essential.

But a deeper challenge is emerging.

Visibility alone is not enough.

We often assume that visibility solves the problem. It does not.

Health systems already contain vast amounts of visible data that never influence decisions.

BVDL’s last step is the one that matters:

Captured

→

Represented

→

Exchanged

→

Interpreted

→

Acted on

A determinant may be:

visible but ignored
visible but not trusted
visible but not actionable
visible but disconnected from policy
visible but incapable of changing outcomes

So perhaps the next grand challenge is not:

How do we make the invisible visible?

but rather:

How do we make the visible actionable?

Perhaps the future of our discipline will be judged not by how much data we collect, but by whether newly visible knowledge leads to better decisions, better policies, and better health.
