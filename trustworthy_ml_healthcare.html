Seminar 1 · Allan Tucker

Privacy, Bias & Drift

Managing the statistical hazards of national healthcare data

Seminar 1 — Overview

National health datasets (e.g. CPRD, MIMIC) are a goldmine for AI, but three forces undermine the models we train on them: privacy, bias and concept drift. Tucker's work, rooted in probabilistic graphical models and generative AI, tackles each in turn:

1. Privacy

Can we share useful data without exposing real patients?

Risk of re-identifying individuals from the data.

2. Bias

Are the data and the models fair and transparent?

Sampling bias gets baked into predictions.

3. Drift

Do models stay valid as populations change?

Data is dynamic, so accuracy decays over time.

IDA group, Brunel University London · AI applied across healthcare, biology & the environment

Privacy — Synthetic Data as a Solution

Rather than sharing real records, generate realistic synthetic data that preserves statistical structure:

  • Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) & probabilistic graphical models
  • Validated for fidelity on CPRD & time-series MIMIC data
  • Causal structure recovered via the FCI algorithm
  • Stress-tested with simulated attacks — matching synthetic records back to real patients

The core trade-off

Utility ↔ Privacy

More privacy protection → less detail. The hardest cases are rare diseases and richly-featured patients, where anonymity and usefulness pull hardest against each other.

Bias & Concept Drift

Dealing with Bias

  • Sampling bias & prejudice get baked into models
  • Not just rebalancing hard datasets — also exposing unwarranted links & the semantics of latent variables
  • Mitigation via boosting; demands transparency
  • Synthetic data can inherit — or amplify — bias

Concept Drift

  • Health data is dynamic — populations and practice shift
  • A model trained once silently decays
  • Drift Detection (DDM) tested on cardiovascular disease
  • Must monitor & capture changing subpopulations

The Building Blocks: HL7 FHIR, toFHIR & onFHIR-feast

HL7 FHIR + CDM

A standard, machine-processable common data model. FHIR Profiling (e.g. an Echocardiogram profile), code systems, value sets & units make the data self-describing.

toFHIR

A mapping language that transforms SQL / source data into FHIR resources. Configurable without custom development, with rich, human-readable metadata.

onFHIR-feast

A feature store (like SageMaker / Hopsworks) defining reusable features via FHIR Search & FHIR Path, going from population → time-series → one tabular dataset.

Built on a Secure Health Data Repository (onFHIR) hosting the standardised data

Federated ML & FAIR Principles

Federated Machine Learning

  • Originated in the FAIR4Health project
  • Extract a dataset at each deployment site
  • Train local models, transfer only model updates to the centre
  • Aggregate into a boosted global model (raw data never moves)

Conforming to FAIR

  • Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable
  • Decouples AI algorithms from the data
  • A repeatable, testable data-prep pipeline
  • Reusable features & datasets across projects and phases (training vs online)

Result: privacy-preserving AI that generalises across hospitals

Bringing the two together

Convergence & Divergence

Two halves of one trustworthy-AI lifecycle

Points of Convergence

Same domain & goal

Both target ML on sensitive national/clinical health data, aiming for AI that is reliable and trustworthy in practice.

Privacy is central

Both keep patients protected: Tucker via synthetic data, Sinaci via federated learning & on-premises data.

Federated mindset

Neither moves raw data to a central store; insight (synthetic data or model updates) travels instead of records.

Real data, reusable

Grounded in real datasets (CPRD, MIMIC; FHIR hospital data) and a shared push for generalizable, reusable outputs.

Together they cover the full journey from raw records to a deployed, trustworthy model.

How They Fit Together

The two seminars are consecutive stages of one trustworthy-AI lifecycle.

Upstream · Sinaci

Get the data right

Standardise, transform & extract features into a reproducible, FAIR, AI-ready pipeline that protects data by design.

Downstream · Tucker

Keep the model honest

On that data, validate privacy, detect & mitigate bias, and monitor concept drift over time.

Good plumbing without governance is unsafe; governance without good data is impossible.

Main Takeaways

  •  Standards are the foundation. FHIR-based interoperability (toFHIR, onFHIR-feast) makes health-ML data reproducible, reusable and decoupled from any single algorithm.
  •  Clean data is necessary but not sufficient. Even well-engineered data carries privacy risk, bias and drift that must be actively managed.
  •  Privacy can be engineered. Synthetic data and federated learning let us extract value while raw patient records stay protected.
  •  Models are never “done”. Bias and concept drift demand transparency and continuous monitoring after deployment.
  •  Trustworthy health AI = pipeline + governance. Sinaci builds the road; Tucker keeps the journey safe.
