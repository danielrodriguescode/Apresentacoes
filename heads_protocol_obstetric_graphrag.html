LLMs alone are unsafe

Large language models answer in natural language but fail clinicians when used unaided:

  • They hallucinate and can't trace an answer to a source.
  • They reason weakly over the multi-step, conditional logic of guidelines.
  • Dense (embedding-only) RAG captures surface similarity — not the structured relations behind clinical reasoning.

Grounding LLMs in ontologies and knowledge graphs sharply reduces hallucination in clinical QA.

Aim and research question

Design, build and rigorously evaluate a graph-RAG decision-support pipeline for obstetric guidelines that grounds answers in a provenance-aware knowledge graph, fuses graph and dense retrieval, cites sources, detects contradictions, and raises escalation flags.

Study I · Scoping review

Map how guideline knowledge is represented, retrieved and reasoned over in CDSS across clinical domains — with obstetrics as the motivating exemplar.

  Method

  • JBI methodology, reported per PRISMA-ScR
  • OSF pre-registration before screening
  • 6 databases · dual independent screening · PCC framework

  Output

  • Cross-domain evidence map
  • Characterisation of evaluation methods
  • The gap that motivates the thesis
Peters et al., 2020 · Tricco et al., 2018
I II III IV V

Study II · Obstetric knowledge graph

Build an obstetric property-graph ontology with explicit guideline versioning and schema-guided triple extraction.

  Construction

  • LLM extraction normalised to SNOMED-CT & ICD-11
  • Explicit vs. inferred edges; provenance on every edge
  • scispaCy extraction baseline

  Layered validation

  • Automated checks over the full graph
  • Stratified ~300–500 triple human review
  • Safety-critical subset signed off by an obstetrician

Metrics: source faithfulness · linking validity · hallucination rate · Cohen/Fleiss κ · precision/recall/F1

I II III IV V

Study III · Graph-RAG architecture

Design and implement the dual-retrieval, fusion and grounded-generation pipeline — isolating each component's contribution by ablation.

  Components

  • Entity linking + intent-aware routing
  • Parallel graph + dense retrieval
  • Reciprocal rank fusion + cross-encoder rerank
  • Cited generation; optional MedGemma fine-tuning

  Controlled ablation

  • 4 configs: vector-only · graph-only · hybrid · hybrid+reranker
  • Single-hop vs. multi-hop
  • Latency, token cost & citation correctness
Technical artefact — no human participants at this stage

Study IV · Evaluation and expert panel

A purpose-built obstetric QA benchmark scored by automated metrics and a blinded obstetrician panel.

~200QA items, balanced across categories

4single-hop · multi-hop · contradiction · escalation

3–5obstetricians & obstetric nurses on the panel

RAGAS (faithfulness, answer relevancy, context precision/recall) + human ratings of correctness, safety & citation. Hypothesis: hybrid graph-RAG outperforms dense-only RAG and unaided LLMs on multi-hop and contradiction queries.

Es et al., 2024 · non-parametric / mixed-effects models · ICC / weighted κ
I II III IV V

Study V · Clinical safety

A graph-based safety layer: escalation flags and inter-guideline contradiction detection.

  Red/amber taxonomy

  • Built from guideline red-flag criteria
  • Rule-based flags vs. free-text LLM warnings
  • Exemplar scenarios as structured case studies

  Contradiction by traversal

  • Detection over versioned, provenance-tagged edges
  • Curated NICE vs. FIGO vs. RCOG benchmark
  • Precision/recall + agreement with expert consensus

Reuses the Study IV obstetrician panel for a usability and safety review

Expected results

  Evidence map

A cross-domain map of knowledge representations for CDSS, quantifying the scarcity of graph-based systems — and the absence of obstetric ones.

  Open artefacts

A released obstetric ontology & populated knowledge graph (Neo4j dump) plus an open QA benchmark, enabling independent replication.

  Quantitative evidence

Whether and when hybrid graph-RAG beats dense-only RAG, with latency and cost trade-offs characterised for realistic deployment.

  Validated safety

Expert-rated safety of escalation flags and accuracy of contradiction detection across NICE, FIGO and RCOG.

Acknowledged limits: coverage bounded by included guidelines · model drift · sample-based validation · retrospective evaluation
