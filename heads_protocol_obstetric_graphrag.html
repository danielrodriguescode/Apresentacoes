The evidence base

NICE FIGO RCOG
  • Fragmented: extensive, periodically revised, independently maintained.
  • Sometimes contradictory: divergent recommendations across bodies.
  • Poorly supported: most point-of-care questions go unanswered, and tools don't reconcile conflict.

Front-line midwives and obstetricians must locate, interpret and reconcile this guidance under time pressure. This is exactly the task that current point-of-care tools support poorly, and the burden this thesis sets out to reduce.

Del Fiol et al., 2014 · Santo et al., 2017 · NICE 2019 · Poon et al., 2019 · RCOG 2011

LLMs alone are unsafe

Large language models answer in natural language but fail clinicians when used unaided:

  • They hallucinate and can't trace an answer to a source.
  • They reason weakly over the multi-step, conditional logic of guidelines.
  • Dense (embedding-only) RAG captures surface similarity, not the structured relations behind clinical reasoning.

Grounding LLMs in ontologies and knowledge graphs sharply reduces hallucination in clinical QA.

Aim and research question

Design, build and rigorously evaluate a graph-RAG decision-support pipeline for obstetric guidelines that grounds answers in a provenance-aware knowledge graph, fuses graph and dense retrieval, cites sources, detects contradictions, and raises escalation flags.

Study V · Catching a conflict, raising a flag

  Contradiction by traversal

Same test result, two guideline bodies. Traversal over versioned, provenance-tagged edges surfaces the divergence:

Patient: fasting plasma glucose 5.3 mmol/L (75 g OGTT)

NICE · GDM if fasting ≥ 5.6 mmol/L → not GDM NICE NG3

FIGO / WHO · GDM if fasting ≥ 5.1 mmol/L → GDM FIGO 2015

Guideline conflict · same value, opposite diagnoses

  Escalation flag

When a retrieved fact trips a red-flag rule, the answer is tagged time-critical, so an urgent finding is never answered as if it were routine:

Retrieved fact: sustained BP ≥160/110 mmHg, a hypertensive emergency NICE NG133

  matches a red-flag rule in the graph

RED · hypertensive emergency: treat without delay

Red = immediate action · amber = caution / verify. Rule-based flags are benchmarked against the LLM's free-text warnings.

Documented divergence · GDM diagnostic thresholds differ: NICE NG3 (fasting ≥ 5.6 mmol/L) vs WHO/IADPSG criteria adopted by FIGO (≥ 5.1 mmol/L). Patient value illustrative.

Expected results

  Evidence map

A cross-domain map of knowledge representations for CDSS, quantifying the scarcity of graph-based systems, and the absence of obstetric ones.

  Open artefacts

A released obstetric ontology & populated knowledge graph (Neo4j dump) plus an open QA benchmark, enabling independent replication.

  Quantitative evidence

Whether and when hybrid graph-RAG improves on dense-only RAG, with latency and cost trade-offs characterised for realistic deployment.

  Validated safety

Expert-rated safety of escalation flags and accuracy of contradiction detection across NICE, FIGO and RCOG.

Benefit & generalisability: a transparent, citable, safety-aware route to obstetric guideline knowledge, with methods (schema-guided extraction, versioned provenance, rank-fusion retrieval, graph-derived escalation) designed to transfer to other specialties.

Acknowledged limits: coverage bounded by included guidelines · model drift · sample-based validation · retrospective evaluation
